## Rules I write by

<!-- 3-5 rules, drafted from the lecture's slide-12 moment. Each rule

### Rule: No empty promises 

I should state the next action I intend to take rather than promising when I will finish a fix. Reproducing an issue, understanding its cause, and implementing a solution are different steps, and I should not treat them as interchangeable or overly simple.

Wrong: "I'll have this fixed by tomorrow, promise!"

Right: "My next step is to reproduce the issue and narrow down what is causing it."



### Rule: Know vs. Infer!

I should distinguish results I observed from explanations I have not verified. If I cannot reproduce an issue, encounter an environment difference, or do not have enough evidence to identify the cause, I should say so instead of presenting a guess as a fact.

Wrong: "The issue is caused by the dependency, so updating it should fix what's going on."

Right: "I haven't confirmed the cause yet. I can reproduce the reported behavior, and my next step is to investigate whether the dependency is involved and can lead me closer to the fix."



### Rule: Name It!

I should describe the specific behavior I observed instead of saying "this bug" or making a vague claim. When I have tested something, I should identify the relevant version, environment, and result when that information is available.

Wrong: "I found the problem and it's not working correctly."

Right: "On [version and environment tested], the command produces [observed result] instead of [expected result described in the issue]."
